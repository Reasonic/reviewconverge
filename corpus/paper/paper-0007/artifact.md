# Maternal mortality: a historical and global picture

## A vast gap
In high-income countries, the maternal mortality ratio was around 11 deaths per
100,000 live births in 2017. In low-income countries it was around 450 per
100,000 — roughly 400 times higher.

## Before modern medicine
The historical toll was staggering. In Sweden and Finland in 1800, around 900
mothers died for every 100,000 live births — nearly one in a thousand. In one
Vienna maternity clinic in the 1840s, every tenth mother died of puerperal fever,
while in a second clinic only one in 5 did.
